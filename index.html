Selected research

EACL 2027Main conferenceAccepted

LegalScope: Measuring Exam-to-Case Transfer in LLM Legal Reasoning

Hongyu Wang, Rilyn R. Han, Yilun Zhao, Xuandong Zhao, and Arman Cohan

We evaluated 28 model configurations on 861 legal-exam questions and 276 lawyer-reviewed case prompts. For each of 138 legal issues, we paired opposing stances on the same facts to test whether a model could argue either side.

Characterizing Oblivious Common-Reward Multi-Player Bandits: Universal Stationarity, Coordination Traps, and Certified Global Rates

Mingxiao Li, Wenhai Lai, and Hongyu Wang

When several learners choose actions independently and see only a shared reward, which guarantees still hold? Our analysis identifies coordination traps and conditions under which the learners can recover global performance guarantees.

Work in progress

First-person video reasoning

I build evaluations that ask whether an object changes a pedestrian’s ability to pass. A matched pair of questions lets us compare noticing motion with understanding what that motion means for the wearer.

How people verify AI-assisted research

I use interviews, think-aloud studies, and experiments to examine what people check before relying on an AI result, and whether the person asked to verify it has the evidence to do so.
